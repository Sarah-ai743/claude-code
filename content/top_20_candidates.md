# Top 20 Candidates
### The strongest of the 100, ranked

Scored out of 10 on five criteria, total out of 50. Ties are broken by hook strength,
then twist quality.

| Criterion | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Does the first line alone stop a thumb? Must be legible in under 2 seconds |
| **Curiosity** | Strength of the "I need to know what happens" pull |
| **Visual** | How much of the mystery can be *shown* rather than narrated |
| **Twist** | Does the reversal recontextualise everything before it, or merely continue it |
| **Rewatch** | Does knowing the ending make the opening better? |

**Production difficulty is deliberately excluded from the ranking** — it's a scheduling
input, not a quality signal. It's listed separately so you can sequence the build.

## Why each one is here

**1. PIX-002 — One Frame Longer.** The purest concept in the database. The premise is
one sentence a five-year-old understands, the anomaly is visible in a progress bar, and
the ending turns a passive object into something that has been waiting. Rewatch value is
maximal: on a second viewing, every play of the file inside the story is the viewer's own
play of the Short.

**2. CAM-009 — From Behind.** The strongest twist in the set. Nested recursion is hard to
write and trivial to *show* — monitors inside monitors need no explanation at all. The
eleventh layer is the best final line in the hundred.

**3. HOME-005 — The Extra Room.** Highest immediate visual legibility of anything here: a
robot vacuum's floor map is already a diagram, and the extra rectangle reads instantly.
Cheap to shoot, ends on a physical reveal rather than an idea.

**4. PH-003 — One Step Closer.** Best hook-to-cost ratio in the database. It's a phone
screen and a hallway, and the escalation is the viewer's own instinct — *delete it* — turned
into the thing that makes it worse. Difficulty 2.

**5. CAM-001 — The Hour Ahead.** Clean prediction premise with a category twist that avoids
the obvious. The camera inside the wall reframes the whole video from "the future" to "the
past has been watching," which is a harder and better turn.

**6. EXP-005 — Still Online.** Emotionally the strongest of the old-hardware concepts. The
final revelation inverts the entire story's direction — he isn't catching up to a
conversation, he's been echoing one — and it's carried by a CRT and a scrolling text window.

**7. PH-019 — Significant Locations.** The last shot (a storage unit wall of identical
phones, all showing his location history) is the single most arresting image in the
hundred. Costs a location and set dressing, and it's worth it.

**8. PIX-004 — Nineteen Ninety-Eight.** Analogue-to-digital contrast gives you free visual
texture, and the reveal implicates the viewer directly without breaking the fourth wall.
Needs period-plausible footage, which is the cost.

**9. EXP-001 — Sold From Your House.** Fastest-comprehension premise here: a marketplace
listing showing your own living room needs zero setup. Twist is slightly softer than the
top tier, which is the only thing keeping it out of the top five.

**10. CAM-004 — Arrivals.** "Thirty-one people walked in. None walked out." is arguably the
best raw hook line in the database — a complete mystery in nine words. The twist is
familiar in shape, which caps it here.

**11. AI-015 — The Delivery Route.** Best final revelation in the set: a house being
rebuilt object by object, two hundred miles away. Difficulty 7 — needs a warehouse
interior — so schedule it once the pipeline is proven.

**12. PH-013 — Twenty Thousand Steps.** Turns a data readout into a horror image through
one scale change: the grid isn't a neighbourhood, it's a floor plan. Easily animated.

**13. MAP-005 — The Walker.** Very high visual potential, and the shadows-pointing-at-the-
camera detail is the kind of seeded clue rewatchers hunt for. Requires convincing
street-imagery plates across nineteen looks.

**14. EXP-004 — February Thirtieth.** The most elegant hook: an impossible date needs no
explanation in any language, which matters for an international audience. Archive-paper
texture is a strong visual world we don't otherwise use.

**15. PH-005 — Half A Second Behind.** Simple, cheap, physically unnerving, and the twist
(not delayed footage — *different* footage) is a genuine category flip rather than an
escalation.

**16. CAM-015 — One Hallway.** Excellent escalating visual: sixty feeds, one corridor.
Slight risk of reading as abstract, so the walked-floor-plan beat has to be concrete.

**17. AI-005 — The Second Speaker.** Difficulty 2 and a curiosity score of 10 — the best
efficiency in the database. The three-speakers ending is the strongest cheap ending here.
Lower visual score because it lives in a transcript panel.

**18. PIX-006 — The Fourth Window.** Video-call grids are instantly readable on a phone,
and the slow brightening of the fourth window is a real visual escalation rather than a
narrated one.

**19. ACC-009 — Seen.** The most emotionally affecting concept in the hundred, and nearly
free to produce. Held back only by visual range — it's read receipts on a screen — so the
sound design has to carry it.

**20. PH-010 — Still Replying.** Strong hook, strong feeling, and the camera-light detail
at the end is a perfect five-second close. Ranked last of the twenty because the
"dead relative still messaging" shape is the most familiar territory the channel touches;
the execution has to stay strictly on the *old phone's parallel thread* to feel new.

## Recommended first five to produce

Ranked by quality **per unit of production cost**, so the pipeline proves itself on
achievable work before the expensive concepts:

1. **PH-003 — One Step Closer** *(difficulty 2)* — phone screen and a hallway plate
2. **AI-005 — The Second Speaker** *(difficulty 2)* — transcript UI and sound design
3. **ACC-009 — Seen** *(difficulty 2)* — message UI, entirely screen-based
4. **PIX-002 — One Frame Longer** *(difficulty 3)* — one room plate, one player UI
5. **CAM-004 — Arrivals** *(difficulty 3)* — doorbell-camera look, one porch

These five cover five different surfaces, five different anomaly classes and five
different twist architectures, so the first batch also road-tests the taxonomy rather
than five variations of one look.

**Hold until the pipeline is proven:** AI-015 (7), PH-019 (6), PIX-004 (6), MAP-005 (6).
